Konnagar

Exploring the Ancient Roots, Culture, and Architectural Marvels of the Hooghly's West Bank

Landmarks & Heritage

The architectural pillars that built the city's historical importance.

Baro Mandir Ghat

Built in 1821

Twelve identical Shiva temples featuring classic Bengal 'Aatchala' architecture. They have served as the spiritual anchor for the local community for over two centuries right on the banks of the Hooghly River.

Shakuntala Kali Temple

Ancient Heart

A deeply revered ancient temple representing the Shakti worship traditions of Bengal. It remains a focal point for major festivals, drawing devotees from across the state.

First Bata Factory (1931)

Industrial Landmark

Before moving to Batanagar, the famous Czechoslovak shoe tycoon Tomáš Baťa established his very first Indian footwear operation right here in Konnagar, making it a critical site in India's industrial history.

Ghoshal Bari

571-Year Tradition

Received Zamindari rights in 1454 during the Mughal era. The family preserves a 571-year-old Durga Puja tradition that perfectly blends ancient rituals with the town's evolving culture.

Find Us on the Map

Located on the picturesque west bank of the Hooghly River.

Study Trails & Educational Roots

Institutions born from the Bengal Renaissance that shaped modern minds.

Konnagar High School

Established 1854

Founded by Shib Chandra Deb, this historic school laid the groundwork for mass literacy and produced legendary alumni, including Shashi Bhusan Chatterjee.

Konnagar Public Library

Established 1858

One of the oldest free reading rooms in the region, established to cultivate intellectual awakening and literary discussions among the local youth.

Nabagram Hiralal Paul College

Established 1957

A prominent general degree college contributing significantly to higher education and youth development in the surrounding Hooghly district.

Civic & Government Centers

The administrative heart driving the town's development.

Konnagar Municipality

Established 1944

Governing an area of 4.67 sq km, the municipality was founded by Nrishinha Das Basu to manage the rapidly growing suburban population. Located at 73, G.T. Road, it remains the civic heart of the city.

Railway & Post Systems

19th Century Logistics

Konnagar's integration into the colonial rail network and the establishment of its post office fundamentally transformed it from a riverside village into a connected economic hub.

Arts, Songs & Literature

Where the melody of the river meets the poetry of Bengal.

Tagore's Melodies at the Ghat

Rabindranath Tagore visited Konnagar multiple times, including a significant trip at age 19 to the Brahmo Samaj Ghat with his father, where the tranquil river influenced his poetic and musical sensibilities.

Baganbari's Literary Inspiration

Abanindranath Tagore’s childhood experiences in Konnagar’s lush gardens inspired his timeless Bengali children’s classics like Kshirer Putul and Buro Angla.

The Sibram Connection

The legendary Bengali humorist and writer Sibram Chakraborty spent a part of his childhood living near G.T. Road beside the High School, absorbing the local culture.

The Expanded Hall of Fame

The historical luminaries who shaped the legacy of Konnagar on a global scale.

Shib Chandra Deb

Modern Architect

A pioneering Brahmo leader who tirelessly brought the railway station, post office, and foundational schools to Konnagar.

Sri Aurobindo

Philosopher & Yogi

A world-renowned yogi and fierce Indian nationalist, whose deep ancestral roots trace directly back to this historic soil.

Abanindranath Tagore

Bengal School of Art

The illustrious artist spent pivotal childhood years in his family's Konnagar Baganbari beside the Hooghly River.

Sisir Kumar Mitra

Pioneer of Radio-Science

An internationally acclaimed physicist born in Konnagar, whose groundbreaking research established radio science in India.

Digambar Mitra

1st Bengali Sheriff

Born in 1817, he was a prominent Derozian and successful businessman who rose to become the first Bengali Sheriff of Kolkata.

Shafiur Rahman

Language Martyr

Born in Konnagar, he sacrificed his life as one of the key martyrs of the historic Bengali Language Movement in 1952.

Sibram Chakraborty

Legendary Humorist

The beloved Bengali writer lived near Konnagar High School during his childhood, absorbing the town's vibrant character.

Jan Antonín Baťa

Industrialist

The shoe magnate chose Konnagar to lay the very first foundations of the Bata Shoe Company in India in 1931.
